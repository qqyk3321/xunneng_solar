PRACTICAL SOLAR EDUCATION

Q: What do solar panels, an inverter and a battery do?
A: Solar panels turn sunlight into electricity. An inverter converts electricity into a form household appliances can use. A battery stores energy for later use. In a compatible hybrid system, solar, battery storage and the electricity grid can work together, subject to the equipment, available energy, settings and power limits.
Antigua explanation: https://www.solortide.com/antigua/how-it-works
Dominica explanation: https://www.solortide.com/dominica/how-it-works

Q: How does solar help during the day? Do I always need a battery to use solar energy?
A: Solar can supply household appliances directly during sunny hours, reducing electricity bought from the grid when generation matches demand. A battery stores surplus energy for later use and can support backup in a suitable system. The amount of storage needed depends on evening consumption and outage priorities; the right configuration requires an assessment.

Q: Can I use solar energy at night?
A: Panels need sunlight to generate electricity. Energy stored in a battery during the day can be used after sunset, within the battery's usable charge, reserve and power limits. If the grid is available, it can supply a shortfall according to the system configuration.
Antigua explanation: https://www.solortide.com/antigua/how-it-works
Dominica explanation: https://www.solortide.com/dominica/how-it-works

Q: Do solar panels work on cloudy days? What about shade?
A: Cloud cover and shade reduce the sunlight reaching the panels and can reduce output. Generation varies with conditions. Trees and nearby buildings can cast changing shadows, so roof layout matters. A battery may cover some shortfall while usable energy and power are available; the grid can also contribute when connected and supported by the configuration.

Q: Will solar keep my electricity on during a power cut?
A: A correctly configured backup system can isolate from the grid and supply selected essentials using available solar and battery energy. Having solar panels alone does not establish that a home has outage backup. Runtime depends on charge, sunlight, the appliances running and system limits. An empty battery with insufficient sunlight cannot sustain backup.
Antigua outage FAQ: https://www.solortide.com/antigua/faq#hybrid-outage
Dominica outage FAQ: https://www.solortide.com/dominica/faq#hybrid-outage

Q: Can I run air conditioning, a refrigerator or the whole house?
A: A suitably sized system can support appliances such as air conditioning, refrigerators, washing machines, lights, TVs and WiFi. This does not mean every appliance can run together or continue throughout an outage. Engineers consider simultaneous loads, appliance starting requirements, inverter power, battery capacity and backup priorities. Ask the team to assess a specific appliance combination.
Antigua appliances FAQ: https://www.solortide.com/antigua/faq#household-appliances
Dominica appliances FAQ: https://www.solortide.com/dominica/faq#household-appliances

Q: How many hours will a battery last?
A: There is no single runtime for every home. It depends on usable stored energy, starting charge, the appliances running, reserve settings and any solar contribution. Website backup previews use stated assumptions; they do not guarantee whole-house runtime. A specific estimate requires the proposed equipment and a list of loads and operating times.
Antigua backup-time FAQ: https://www.solortide.com/antigua/faq#backup-duration
Dominica backup-time FAQ: https://www.solortide.com/dominica/faq#backup-duration

Q: What is the difference between kW and kWh?
A: kW measures power: how quickly electricity is being generated or used at a particular moment. kWh measures energy: how much electricity is generated, stored or used over time. Inverter power and battery energy capacity answer different questions. Battery capacity alone does not tell you which appliances can run together or guarantee backup hours.

Q: Should I choose a larger battery or more solar panels?
A: Panels generate energy; batteries store it. A larger battery alone does not generate more electricity. Daytime-heavy use may need less storage, while evening-heavy use can make storage more useful. Panels still need to produce enough surplus to recharge the battery. The team balances usage, roof space, generation, backup needs and budget rather than assuming the largest system is best.

Q: How do my roof and electricity habits affect the system?
A: Roof direction, slope, available space and shade affect generation. East-facing panels tend to produce earlier in the day and west-facing panels later. Using suitable appliances during sunny hours can help use solar energy directly. Share when you use major loads and what you want backed up so the team can plan around your routine.

Q: What happens when the battery reaches its reserve? Can solar and the grid work together?
A: The reserve is energy held back according to system settings; reaching it does not necessarily mean the battery is empty. With the grid available, grid electricity can supply a shortfall when solar is insufficient. Compatible hybrid systems can combine sources, but operation depends on configuration and available power. Electricity bought from the grid still incurs normal charges.

Q: What is an LFP or LiFePO4 battery?
A: LiFePO4, also called LFP, means Lithium Iron Phosphate, a lithium-ion battery chemistry. Sol & Tide's published home-storage information describes LFP batteries for storing energy for household use and backup. Ask the team for the specifications and terms of the proposed model.

SUPPORT AND PROJECT QUESTIONS

Q: Can you integrate an existing generator or expand my current system?
A: In many cases these options can be assessed. Generator integration depends on the model, existing electrical setup, property needs and site assessment. Expansion depends on the existing design, compatible equipment, roof space and electrical capacity. Compatibility is not confirmed until engineers review the specific installation.

Q: Is there a software or monitoring subscription fee?
A: The published company FAQ says software services and the monitoring platform are included at no additional charge with Sol & Tide equipment. Remote monitoring requires a working WiFi network with internet access and connected equipment. Ask the team to confirm the monitoring arrangements for the proposed model.

Q: Do you provide cleaning, reinforcement or replacement parts?
A: Sol & Tide lists panel cleaning, reinforcement and Local Parts Stock among its services. The team must agree the service scope and confirm availability for the particular part and system model. This knowledge base does not show live stock or confirm a service appointment.

Q: How long does installation take? What warranty, certification or utility approval applies?
A: Installation timing varies with equipment availability, site preparation and any required approvals. Ask for a schedule and the exact warranty terms with the proposal. Equipment certificates and standards depend on the region and selected model; ask the team for the applicable documents. Certification references do not replace project-specific utility connection approval. No universal installation deadline, warranty duration or approval guarantee is supplied here.
